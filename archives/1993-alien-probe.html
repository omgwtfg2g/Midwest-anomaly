ROCKFORD MAN CLAIMS ALIENS PROBED HIM; SAYS HE MAY NEVER SIT AGAIN

Shaken resident describes a terrifying examination aboard a spacecraft; says his complaints were ignored.
BY THE MIDWEST ANOMALY · OCTOBER 23, 1993
Illustration depicting the reported visitors. No photographs of the encounter were produced.

GRAND RAPIDS, MICH. — A Rockford man says he has slept standing up for eleven nights after what he describes as a horrifying medical examination aboard an unidentified flying object. His wife says he now flinches whenever anyone mentions an annual checkup.

According to the man's account, three small gray visitors entered his bedroom shortly after 2 a.m., communicated entirely through blinking, and informed him that he had been selected for “an important scientific procedure.”

“I kept screaming that they had the wrong man,” he recalled. “One of them looked at a clipboard and said, ‘No, we don’t.’ That's when I knew I was in serious trouble.”

The alleged examination reportedly took place aboard a circular craft hovering over a nearby cornfield. The man described a spotless room, a bright overhead light and a clipboard that appeared to be made of liquid metal.

“They told me it was a routine examination. I've been to the doctor before. Nobody's ever needed a tractor beam.”

The resident claims the visitors used an unfamiliar instrument during the examination, then refused to explain what it measured. When he requested a copy of his medical records, one alien allegedly handed him a small glowing cube that played a recording of a cow mooing.

“The sheriff asked if I could identify the suspects. I said they're three feet tall, gray, and one of them owes me an apology.”

Neighbors reported no unusual activity. One described the man as ‘usually very friendly’ but said he had recently refused a complimentary seat at the local diner. A waitress said he ate his breakfast standing beside the counter and left when someone offered him a stool.

The man has demanded a written apology, reimbursement for a missing pair of slippers, and a signed guarantee that no further examinations will be scheduled. Asked whether he would cooperate with additional extraterrestrial research, he turned pale and requested that the reporter stop using the word ‘additional.’

His final concern remains unanswered. “They told me to come back in six months,” he said, gripping the edge of the kitchen counter. “I'm not going anywhere near that cornfield. And I'm never buying another telescope.”
