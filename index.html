Cornfield Mystery Revives Bizarre 1957 Alien-Jelly Abduction Legend

A strange metallic object in a rural field has inspired a wildly unlikely theory involving an extraterrestrial woman, a Brazilian farmer and a substance nobody wants to identify.
By The Midwest Anomaly   |   October 16, 2026
Artist's impression of a mysterious object in a field. Not a photograph of the reported discovery.

STEVENS COUNTY — Reports of an unusual metallic object discovered in a cornfield have sparked an unexpected revival of one of UFO folklore's most peculiar tales: the alleged 1957 abduction of Brazilian farmer Antônio Vilas-Boas.

Vilas-Boas claimed to have encountered an unusual craft and an extraterrestrial woman. His account later became notorious for its intimate details and descriptions of an oily, gel-like substance.

Self-appointed anomalous-materials expert Dr. Harold Venn claims the events may be connected through what he calls “interstellar adhesive residue.”

“The question isn't why they came back. The question is whether they brought more jelly.”

Venn's team has assigned the case the name Operation Sticky Situation, pending further examination of absolutely no evidence whatsoever.

A source described as a retired tractor mechanic reportedly asked whether anyone had considered that the object might simply be farm equipment. His suggestion was immediately rejected for being insufficiently mysterious.

As online speculation grows, the purported investigators remain divided over one crucial question: if extraterrestrials really have returned after nearly seventy years, are they looking for answers — or a second date?
